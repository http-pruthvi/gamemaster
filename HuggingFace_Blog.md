# Bridging Storytelling and Strategy: Building a Self-Improving AI Gamemaster with OpenEnv

## The Vision
In the world of tabletop RPGs, a Gamemaster (GM) must balance two conflicting roles: a creative storyteller and a rigorous rules engine. While Large Language Models (LLMs) are incredible at the former, they often fail at the latter—hallucinating dice rolls, forgetting inventory, and ignoring health points.

For the **Meta PyTorch OpenEnv Hackathon**, I built **AI Gamemaster**, a self-improving agent system designed to master the complexity of world modeling and long-horizon rule enforcement.

## The Problem: The "Hallucinating" GM
Baseline LLMs often "cheat" or "forget" when playing games. They might narrate a heroic victory even when the internal dice roll was a failure. This creates a "desync" between the narrative and the game state. To solve this, I needed an environment that could programmatically reward an AI for being mechanically accurate.

## The Solution: A Deterministic OpenEnv World
Using Meta's **OpenEnv** framework, I developed a Micro-RPG engine that acts as the "Source of Truth." 

- **The Environment:** A Python-based server that tracks objective state (HP, Inventory, Locations).
- **The Observation:** The AI receives the player's input and a "system dice roll" (an objective d20 roll generated by the engine).
- **The Action:** The AI must output a JSON object containing both the `narrative_response` and programmatic `state_updates` (e.g., `damage_amount`).

### The Reward Logic (The Heart of Self-Improvement)
The secret to the agent's improvement is a strict reward rubric:
- **+1.0** for correctly applying damage on a successful roll.
- **-1.0** for applying damage on a miss (Hallucination).
- **-2.0** for failing to follow the JSON schema (Formatting).

## Training for Mastery with Unsloth & GRPO
To move beyond a baseline model, I used **Unsloth** and **TRL's GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)**. GRPO is particularly powerful here because it allows the model to sample multiple "narrative/action" pairs and learn which ones better satisfy the rules of the game engine without needing a separate reward model.

The result is a model that **self-improves** through interaction. It learns that to get a high reward, it must subordinate its creative narrative to the objective reality of the dice.

## Results
- **Initial State:** The model wrote great stories but ignored the `system_dice_roll` 70% of the time.
- **After Training:** The model achieved **95%+ rule adherence**, correctly updating the game state even in complex, multi-turn combat scenarios.

## Why This Matters
This project demonstrates that we can use RL and frameworks like OpenEnv to train agents that are not just "chatty," but are capable of high-fidelity tool use and consistent world modeling—the foundation for the next generation of AI agents in gaming, enterprise workflows, and beyond.
